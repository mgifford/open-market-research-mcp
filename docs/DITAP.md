# DITAP curriculum alignment

## Recommended pilot

DITAP Core Curriculum, **Module 2, Sprint 4: Conducting Effective Market Research**:
https://github.com/usds/ditap-curriculum-update/tree/main/3_Curriculum/3A_DITAP-Core-Curriculum/Module-2/Sprint-4

The exercise supplements, and does not replace, the existing syllabus or agency-specific instruction.

## Learner scenario

A fictional federal program needs to modernize case management. The team must define intended users and tasks, search the market broadly, recognize government reuse and open-source alternatives, request appropriate ACR evidence, and document uncertainties.

## Zero-install classroom start

Open the published site and use **Try a fictional DITAP example**, or choose **Start new research**. No installation or account is needed. Ask learners to save a research file before closing the browser; **Continue existing research** reopens it. Optional local MCP and AI guidance are on the **About** page and are not prerequisites.

## 45-minute workshop plan

1. (5 minutes) Define the service need, the agency context, and the affected users.
2. (10 minutes) Search source directories and decide which live systems should be queried externally. Note that the starter does not run these searches itself.
3. (10 minutes) Create at least three research options across different approaches. Use fictional names or defensible published sources; do not imply a result was independently verified.
4. (10 minutes) Record any available ACR links, product versions, partial claims, and planned testing. Identify missing evidence, and generate an appropriate follow-up question.
5. (10 minutes) Export the Markdown report, critique its omissions, and identify which specialist or source is needed next.

## Outcomes

A learner can:

- Formulate neutral market research questions based on user needs.
- Identify official contracting data and government software inventory sources.
- Distinguish vendor claims from independent evaluations.
- Explain why absent ACR evidence is not itself a finding of nonconformance.
- Recognize that exceptions require documented, authorized agency decisions.
- Distinguish a reference link from a performed search and a no-result search from a failed search.
- Trace each finding to evidence and keep contradictory records separate.
- Produce transparent, reviewable working notes rather than an AI-issued recommendation.

## Assessment rubric (0-2 points per dimension)

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| User needs | Product-first | Partly user-centered | Clearly defines users and outcomes |
| Breadth of market | One supplier | Two approaches | Government reuse, open source, and market alternatives |
| Evidence provenance | Unsourced | Some citations | Source links and stated limitations |
| Accessibility | No evidence review | ACR exists | Version/scope review plus gap and testing questions |
| Acquisition judgment | AI conclusion | Some caveats | Human decision points and exceptions distinguished |

A perfect score is not an actual procurement or accessibility approval.

## Extensions

- Instructors can use SAM.gov and USAspending through existing 1102tools MCPs in a separate approved AI environment.
- Instructors can compare an ART-generated requirements set with vendor ACR claims.
- As a follow-up, evaluate the RFI questions learners would ask without privileging incumbents.
- Include small business access, open-source project governance, reuse rights, accessibility, interoperability, and long-term maintenance.
